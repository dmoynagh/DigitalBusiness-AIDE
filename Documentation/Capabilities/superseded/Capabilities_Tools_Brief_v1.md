# Capabilities Tools — Brief

> **Version 1** (2026-08-27). First issuance. Establishes the scope and purpose of the Tools
> subtopic within Capabilities.
>
> Created: 2026-08-27 | Last modified: 2026-08-27

---

## Purpose

The Tools subtopic owns the model for how tools are defined, structured, and published. It
provides the governing design so that any domain can produce tools that are consistent,
reportable, and publishable across platforms.

A tool removes the need to decide — it exists where judgment adds nothing and determinism adds
everything (`D20`, role and purpose of a tool). The Tools subtopic defines what a tool must
contain, how it declares its scope, how it handles inputs and failures, and what it reports.

---

## What the Tools subtopic produces

**A tools design** — how tool documents are structured, what they contain, and how they behave.
This feeds the Standards Standard (which governs production of both standards and tools) and
any future Tools Standard if the design warrants a standalone published artefact.

---

## What the Tools subtopic does not own

- **Individual tools** produced by other domains — those domains own their own tools;
  this subtopic provides the rules they follow.
- **The scope model** — shared with Standards at the Capabilities level (`D18`, two-layer scope).
- **Versioning and currency** — shared concern, examined under one lens (`Q7`).
- **Migration** — scoped to standards only; tools are excluded as no case currently exists.

---

## Success signals

- A domain author can produce a well-structured tool document by following this subtopic's
  design, without needing to invent structure or conventions.
- A session can read a tool document and know unambiguously: what the tool does, what it needs,
  what it will report, and when to stop and hand back.
- The publisher can extract and deploy a tool from its design without judgment calls.
